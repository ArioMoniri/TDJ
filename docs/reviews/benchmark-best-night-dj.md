# Benchmark review: the featured golden-era night against published sets A–K

*Same reviewer and rubric as rounds 8–11. Inputs: `docs/specs/round11-best-night.md` (the shipped night after the round-11 hand-fixes and the three benchmark edits), `docs/reviews/benchmark-reference-sets.md` (sets A–K, Demirbaş 2018, Victor Tran, Lavocah), `docs/reviews/round11-best-night-dj.md`. Recording existence, years, energy and fame for every proposed side were checked against `data/index/songs.json`, `data/knowledge/track_energy.json` and the `famousRecordings` corpus in `data/knowledge/reference_sets.json`. Only this file was written.*

**Headline.** **85/100.** The round-11 hand-fixes landed (D'Agostino out of the pre-summit chair, Demare dropped, *Yo soy de San Telmo* opens the peak milonga, *La bruja* lifts the Echagüe tanda, cortinas back to 43–47 s). Of the three benchmark edits, Pugliese instrumental is a clear gain, Donato instrumental is neutral, and Rodríguez instrumental is a loss. On paper the night now sits **level with Set A (Rauscher, Charlotte) at the top of the golden-era group, and ahead of C, D and I**. Three tanda swaps (§4) take it to **88** and make it the strongest golden-era night in the sample. One honest limit applies: every published set was played live while the DJ read the room. This is a paper comparison of running orders, and the benchmark has no energy or fame data to compare.

Running time check: the listed side and cortina durations add up to 3 h 47 min, plus about 3 min of Cumparsita, so about **3 h 50 min**. The spec header says 4.11 h. Hour shares below use the summed durations: hour 1 = tandas 0–5, hour 2 = 6–11, hour 3 = 12–16, hour 4 = 17–21.

## 2. The three instrumental swaps, item by item

The trigger was Tran's guideline, which the benchmark's own summary records: "at least one third of tandas each hour should be instrumental". Before the edits, hours 2 and 3 were at 13 % and 0 %. After them, hour 2 has 3/5 instrumental tandas and hour 3 has 1/5 (about 21 % of its time). Two of the three swaps landed in hour 2, the hour that needed only one. The hour that was really short (hour 3) got only Pugliese.

1. **Tanda 6, Rodríguez/Moreno → Rodríguez instrumental 1940–42: wrong. Revert it.**
   - Rodríguez's place on a floor is Moreno. Rodríguez has a tanda in 8/11 benchmark sets, and every one of them is Moreno 1938–42.
   - The catalogue holds only six Rodríguez instrumental tangos from 1940–44 (*La torcacita, El morochito, Florida, Jueves, Expresión campera, Argentino soy*). This tanda uses four of them, including fame-0 *Expresión campera*.
   - It was not needed. Once tanda 10 went back to instrumental, hour 2 already has Biagi vals + Donato (2/5 tandas).
   - Mark: 5. With Moreno: 8.
2. **Tanda 10, Donato back to instrumental 1936–40: right, at a small cost. Keep it.**
   - *El lengue, El chamuyo, La tablada* are floor-known. Donato instrumentals have precedent (A: Gutiérrez/instrumental, K: instrumental).
   - The jaunty 1930s colour changes the mood after Echagüe.
   - The cost is the plateau: e62 against a target of 82, the same −20 the round-11 review flagged. The vocal Donato–Lagos tanda would hold the floor slightly better, but it would leave hour 2 with only the Biagi vals as instrumental.
   - Mark: 6.
3. **Tanda 16, Pugliese/Morán → Pugliese instrumental 1944–46: right. This is the best edit of the three.**
   - 1943–49 Pugliese instrumentals are what the benchmark's DJs play (D, E, H, J).
   - At 0.77, before the peak milonga and the summit, the chair wants drive, not Morán's sentiment.
   - *La Yumba* (1946) is inside the 1935–46 window.
   - Nit (an order change, not a swap): *La Yumba* at e84 opening, followed by three e74 sides, sags after the first side. Closing on it (*El arranque, Tierra querida, El remate, La Yumba*) builds into the Láurenz milonga. Either order is defensible.
   - Mark: 8.

**Recommendation for the app:** treat Tran's third as a *night* target (≥ 25–30 % instrumental tandas) plus "≥ 1 instrumental tango or vals tanda per hour". Do not apply it as a hard per-hour third. No published set in the sample meets the per-hour rule, and chasing it here cost a canon vocal tanda.

## 3. Turkish floor (Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir): all-Turkish cortinas, plus or risk?

**What the evidence says** (from the benchmark file only):
- Demirbaş 2018 interviewed Turkish tango DJs. Their preferred cortinas are swing, jazz, Western classical and Latin dance music. Turkish pop or rock is not mentioned.
- The Turkish-linked lists use international pop and rock: H has no Turkish cortinas, J has 2 Turkish cues among about 20, and K rotates 1 Turkish cue.
- The benchmark's own conclusion: an all-Turkish set "has no published precedent found".

**My read as a working DJ** (judgement, not a sourced claim):
- A cortina's job is to empty the floor and be ignored. On a Turkish floor, Turkish songs are the opposite of ignorable.
  - The club hits (*Şımarık*, *Araba*) invite people to stay on the floor and dance or sing along.
  - The emotional classics (*Üsküdar'a Gider İken*, Zeki Müren) carry their own associations into the next tanda.
  - Twenty-one of them in a row read as a theme night, not seasoning.
- For a festival crowd in Istanbul, a few Turkish cues are a welcome local signature, and the international guests enjoy them.
- So all-Turkish is a **risk on a regular local milonga** and a **plus only as an announced theme** ("Türk gecesi").

**Recommendation the app can follow:**
- **Default for a Turkish floor:** about **1/3 Turkish** (7 of 21 cues). Place them at structural moments.
- Fill the rest from the swing, jazz, Latin and chanson pool in `packages/core/src/cortinas.ts`, energy-matched.
- Never use the `danceable-risk` / club Turkish hits.
- No artist more than twice in a night. Today Sezen Aksu appears ×4.
- **All-Turkish:** opt-in theme only.
