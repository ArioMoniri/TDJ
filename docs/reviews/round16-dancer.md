### The `sideOrder` rule as written penalises good DJ practice

Two of its tests go against what good DJs actually do:
- "Never the slowest side first": many tandas open on their best-known, unhurried side (Como Dos Extraños, Malena, Silueta Porteña).
- "The best-loved side last or third": many DJs open with the hook.

These two tests produce 13 of the 14 `sideOrder` items above.

**Replace them with checks for what actually hurts on the floor:**
- (a) Opener fame ≥ 3 and ≥ the tanda's median fame − 1.
- (b) Last side fame ≥ the tanda's max fame − 1, and last-side energy ≥ the tanda's median − 5. A tanda must not fade out.
- (c) In the última and in summits, the last side is the highest-energy side or within 3 of it.
- (d) Flag a dip mid-tanda only when it is ≥ 15 and the side has fame ≤ 2.
- Keep "no title twice in a row" and "no back-and-forth across years".

## 7. Final verdict

**Yes. As the strictest milonguera, I am happy with seed 194 as it stands.** It opens like Buenos Aires, it keeps the cycle, both summits deliver, it has no cliff and no hole, and it closes with the right última and Cumparsita. I would dance to the end and leave happy. The two orders I would change (the última's last side and the fading Di Sarli/Durán tanda, A5 and A4) are things I would mention to the DJ afterwards, not to the organiser.

Seed 12 is a near-yes: fix A1 (the Demare tanda) and the Troilo milonga at M17. Seed 180 is a no until its warm-up (A2) and last summit (A3) are fixed.

Most of the engine's remaining should-fix items are over-strict, as §4 explains. Before the next round, rework `sideOrder`, `cuePopHour`, `cueDramatic`, `rhythmicRun`, and the vals exemptions in `energyStep` and `firstVals`.
