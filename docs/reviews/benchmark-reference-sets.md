# Reference milonga sets for benchmarking (golden era, 4 h, Turkish cortinas)

Compiled 2026-11-10 from web sources only. Every tanda below is transcribed from a published source (blog post, PDF, Spotify playlist). Nothing is invented. Where a source labels something oddly (Spotify metadata, for example), it is kept as published and flagged.

Abbreviations: T = tango, V = vals, M = milonga, X = non-tango dance set (salsa, blues, chacarera, foxtrot), I = instrumental, Voc = with singer, Mix = mixed vocal and instrumental or mixed orchestras. "Pos" means a tanda's position divided by the number of dance tandas (0 is the start, 1 is the end).

## Honesty summary: what was and was not found

- **Turkish sets.** I found **no** published tanda-by-tanda set list tied to a dated milonga or festival in Istanbul, Ankara or Izmir. That includes Istanbul Tango Ritual, tanGO TO Istanbul, Sultans of Istanbul, the International Ankara Tango Festival and the Izmir Tango Marathon. Festival listings name DJs, but none links to playlists. The Mixcloud API returned no Turkish milonga sets ("milonga istanbul/ankara/izmir" gave 0 hits). Facebook and Instagram are not readable without login.
- What **does** exist on the Turkish side:
  1. A Spotify "milonga night" list by a verifiable Turkish tango professional, **Kerem Öksüz** (Set H).
  2. An anonymous Spotify "playlist with cortinas" from a Turkish-named account that uses Turkish pop cortinas (Set J). The owner is unverified.
  3. A set published by a Turkish-named DJ in **Hamburg** (Şemsettin Koçak), which uses a Turkish cortina (Set K). It is diaspora, not a Turkish milonga.
  4. A peer-reviewed Turkish sociology paper based on interviews with Turkish tango DJs. It is the best evidence on Turkish cortina and set conventions.
- **I could not verify** set lists from tango-dj.at. The site is an archive and database association run by Bernhard Gehberger, its newsletters are CD catalogues, and I found no published sets. The same goes for Michael Lavocah (articles only), Antti Suniala (single tandas on "Tanda of the Week", not whole nights; the blog returned 503/404 during fetch) and Keith Elshaw (nothing found). Mixcloud no longer exposes tracklists through its public API: every long milonga mix I checked returned 0 sections.
- Festival DJ names that turned up, as **leads only** (no sets found): Figen Moran and Aykut Dokur (Istanbul Tango Ritual 2008, per Tango-L); Ramo & Gogo (Turkish DJs, pictured in Demirbaş 2018); Tango Mania Istanbul 2026 schedule listing Sercan Yiğit, Mert Ergün, Emre Duyu and Berkil Ceran; the Sultans of Istanbul 2024 "Sultanic Symphony" DJ group (names were garbled in the source, so I have not reproduced them).

## Turkish cortina and set conventions (evidence)

**Demirbaş, A. T. (2018). "Milonganın Eşik Bekçileri: Tango DJ'leri". *Sosyoloji Dergisi* 38(1): 163–178.** DOI 10.26650/SJ.38.1.0001. PDF: https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/643084 . The paper draws on 2016 interviews with Turkish tango DJs, one of whom had about 15 years' experience.

- Tango DJing in Turkey started around 2006–07. Before that, organisers or teachers chose music "gelişigüzel" (haphazardly).
- The structure is "2 tanda tango, 1 tanda vals, 2 tanda tango, 1 tanda milonga". Tango tandas have 4 songs and vals and milonga tandas have 3.
- **Cortinas:** at least 30 s and at most 1–1.5 min of non-tango music. "Swing, caz, klasik Batı müziği, latin dans müzikleri tercih edilen cortina müzikleri arasında yer alır" (swing, jazz, Western classical and Latin dance music are among the preferred cortina music). **Turkish pop or rock is not mentioned as a convention.**
- All interviewed DJs rejected a fully pre-built playlist. They prepare coherent tandas and order them live while reading the floor, over a roughly **4-hour milonga**.
- Tanda coherence rules: same orchestra; close recording dates; no vocal track inside an instrumental tanda; care with the singer.
- **Energy arc:** "Daha yavaş tempoda müzikler ile başlayan milonga gecenin yarısına doğru doruk noktalarına ulaşır." The milonga starts slower and peaks towards the middle of the night. The end may taper again or, conversely, end high.
- Quoted DJ: a beloved track played at the wrong time goes unnoticed, so build up to the tanda meant to be the peak.
- Quoted DJ: "Önemli olan dansçıya göre çalmaktır" (what matters is playing for the dancers, not rare or difficult tracks).

**Observed in Turkish-linked playlists (H, J, K):**
- Cortinas are predominantly **international pop, rock, soul and hip-hop**: Amy Winehouse, 50 Cent, Ed Sheeran, James Brown, Elvis, Despacito, R.E.M., Green Day, Rosemary Clooney, Macarena.
- **Turkish** music appears only as an occasional accent: Can Kazaz "Kendi Halimde" and Yalın "Keşke" (Turkish pop, Set J); Mercan Dede "Güneş Doğudan Doğar" (Turkish Sufi-electronic, rotated as a cortina in Set K); Müge Zeren "İlk Bakışta Quero" as a special track before the final tanda (Set H).
- **Conclusion:** a set made entirely of Turkish cortinas would be distinctive, not conventional. The documented Turkish norm is international genres, with Turkish pop as a minority flavour.

## Explicit statements by DJs on what makes a great set

- **Michael Lavocah** (milonga.co.uk), "Tango DJing Part 3: Shaping the evening", https://www.todotango.com/english/history/chronicle/510/Tango-DJing-Part-3:-Shaping-the-evening/
  - "The DJ will probably want to start gently and build the milonga to some sort of climax … perhaps with a subsidiary climax earlier in the evening."
  - "The flow of tandas must be coherent too."
  - A big contrast every tanda is "too much" for him.
  - The final tanda should be special: energetic, intensely lyrical, or occasionally intimate.
  - Part 2 (tanda construction): https://www.todotango.com/english/history/chronicle/483/Tango-Djing-Part-2:-Tanda-construction
- **Victor Tran**, "Tango DJ Fundamentals Part 2", https://victortrantango.blogspot.com/2022/07/tango-dj-fundamentals-part-2-managing.html
  - TTVTTM is roughly a 1-hour cycle. Cycles run Early (build), Middle (highest intensity), Late (cool down; drop the milonga tanda).
  - Within a cycle, the milonga tanda is the peak and the vals is second.
  - No orchestra or singer in more than one of any 3 consecutive tandas. At least one third of tandas each hour should be instrumental.
  - Pugliese is kept out of the early cycle. Late-1950s Pugliese is "the natural place" for T3 of the late cycle.
  - Last tanda: De Angelis late-50s instrumentals if energy is high, slower Canaro if low, or Di Sarli late-40s/50s instrumentals.
  - La Cumparsita closes the night, either as the last song of the final tanda or as its own 1–2 song set.
- **Dmitry Pruss** (Humilitan blog, Missoula/Salt Lake) self-critiques flow in Set F: "wake-up call of high-drive classic tangos" after a lyrical tanda.
- **Clint Rauscher** (Tangology101): "I generally start from an existing playlist and then make changes" according to the dancers. https://tangology101.com/main.cfm/id/58
- **Paul Lohman**: tandas flow by character; "A simple song from the 1920's is not followed by some dramatic Pugliese from the 1950's." https://paullohman.com/tango-tango-vals-tango-tango-milonga/

## Common patterns

**Sample:** 11 sets (A–K). Normalised "per 4 h" figures use stated or estimated length. Sets E, F, G and J include non-traditional segments; those are excluded from the dance-tanda counts. Small sample, and mostly English-speaking or Northern European scenes. Treat the figures as ranges, not targets.

### 1. D'Arienzo tandas per 4 h (all rhythms)

| Set | A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I | J | K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D'Arienzo tandas | 4 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 0 |
| ≈ per 4 h | 3.8 | 1.0 | ~3 | ~3 | ~0.9 | 1.3 | 1.2 | ~2 | 3.2 | ~1 | 0 |

- **Median ≈ 1.3, mean ≈ 1.9, range 0–3.8.**
- The four "classic golden-era-only" nights (A, C, D, I) run **3–4 D'Arienzo tandas per 4 h**, usually across T, V and M rhythms (A: 3 T + 1 V; C: T + M + V; I: T + V + M).
- When D'Arienzo appears more than once, the tandas are spread out, never adjacent. The rhythms and eras rotate: Echagüe 1938–39, Mauré 1941–44, instrumentals 1935–37.

### 2. Distinct orchestras (with their own tanda)

A 13, B 14, C 15, D 13, E ~19, F ~12, G 14, H ~14, I 13 (partial), J ~17, K ≥10 (partial).

- **Median ≈ 14** per night, typically from 18–26 dance tandas.
- Repeats are common: 2–3 tandas for the top 4–5 orchestras, separated by at least 3 tandas.
- One exception: K plays Demare twice back-to-back.

### 3. Vals and milonga share (of dance tandas)

| Set | A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I | J | K |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vals % | 15 | 15 | 17 | 19 | 12 | 11 | 16 | 6 | 16 | 10 | 15 |
| Milonga % | 15 | 20 | 17 | 14 | 4 | 11 | 11 | 11 | 24 | 14 | 10 |

- **Median vals ≈ 15%, milonga ≈ 14%.** Strict TTVTTM gives 16.7% each.
- TTVTTM is explicit in A, B, C, D and G and in Demirbaş (Turkey). I drifts to TTM TTV; H under-plays vals.
- Vals and milonga tandas have 3 songs in every set. Tango tandas have 4 songs except in A, E, F and I.

### 4. Pugliese position

- Present in **7 of 11** (B, D, E, F, G, H, J), **always exactly once**. Absent from A, C, I (first 3.8 h) and K (partial).
- Positions: 0.48 (D), 0.68 (G), 0.76 (J), 0.89 (H), 0.90 (B), 0.95 (F), 1.0 (E). **Median 0.89**, so Pugliese sits in the **last third**, usually 1–3 tandas before the close.
- It is often preceded by a ramp: Di Sarli 1950s instrumentals (F), D'Arienzo (H), De Angelis instrumentals (E).
- This matches Victor Tran's guidance: late-1950s Pugliese as late-cycle T3, never in the early cycle.

### 5. Ending and La Cumparsita

- Sets with an observable ending: A, B, D, E, F, G, H, J (8). **Cumparsita closes 7/8 (88%).** The exception is B, where the night ran into an alternative block.
- **How it closes:**
  - As the last track of the final tanda: A (D'Arienzo instrumental tanda + D'Arienzo 1951), F (De Angelis instrumental + De Angelis 1961), G (Forever Tango), H and J (Solo Tango).
  - As a standalone track after the last tanda: D (D'Arienzo 1963 after a Castillo vals tanda), E (Laurenz-Maffia 1926 after Pugliese).
- **Last orchestra:** D'Arienzo ×2 (A, D), De Angelis (F), Pugliese (E), modern orchestras (G, H, J).
- **Last tanda energy is bimodal:** either high (D'Arienzo or De Angelis instrumentals) or dramatic (Pugliese or Pugliese-style modern). Victor Tran and Lavocah both describe these options.

### 7. Cortina style

- **International sets:** short excerpts of soul, funk, disco and swing (A, B); 1920s pop (I); Barry White only (G); Soviet and Russian pop (E, F).
- **Rotation:** one cortina reused (G, I, K blocks) or each one different (A, J).
- **Turkish context:** documented norm is swing, jazz, classical and Latin (Demirbaş). Turkish-linked lists use international pop and hip-hop. **Turkish pop or Sufi cortinas appear only as accents** (J: Can Kazaz, Yalın; K: Mercan Dede; H: one Turkish song as a special track, not a cortina).
- **Implication:** an all-Turkish-cortina set has no published precedent found. Treat it as a stylistic choice, not a benchmarked convention.

### 8. Energy-peak placement

- **Stated by DJs:** gentle start, climax around the middle (Demirbaş's Turkish DJs) or later. Lavocah describes a subsidiary climax before the main one. Victor Tran puts the highest intensity in the middle cycles and a cool-down plus emotional close in the last cycle.
- **Observed:** rhythmic peaks (D'Arienzo or Biagi clusters) at about 0.6–0.85 of the night (C, H, A). The dramatic peak (Pugliese) is at about 0.9.
